# Bots work on goals in the background

Status: accepted. Builds on 0058, 0059, 0065, 0066 and 0067. The model is the Anvia harness's goals: `getGoal`, `startGoal` and `updateGoal`, rounds that the app wakes the bot for, and checks that are shell commands on the bot's computer.

## Context

A bot did one thing in the reply to one message. Work that is bigger than a reply (port a parser, write a report from many sources) had to be driven by the person saying "keep going". The harness lets a bot start a goal and then works it round after round with nobody there; the person asked for bots to work like that, except for the harness's coding sessions and approvals.

## Decision

- **Three tools on every bot**: `get_goal`, `start_goal` and `update_goal`, one runtime call, `bot.goal` (wire version 32), with an operation, answered by the reply's own sink and always for the reply's own bot. `start_goal` takes an objective (up to 20,000 characters, with everything a later round needs, since each round starts from the bot's notes and its computer's files), up to eight `checks` (shell commands) and a round cap (`maxRounds`, 20 by default, 100 at most). `update_goal` takes `complete`, `blocked` (with a reason), `pause`, `resume` or `clear`.
- **A bot has one goal** (`bot_goals`, unique by bot): an unfinished one (active, paused or blocked) is never replaced; a complete or cleared one is, in one upsert, so two goals started at once leave one. Each action is taken only from where it makes sense (`pause` from active, `resume` from paused or blocked, and so on) and a resume of a goal that ran out of rounds gives it `GOAL_DEFAULT_ROUNDS` (20) more, while one that stopped with rounds left keeps its cap.
- **The control plane drives the rounds.** A reply that ends with nothing waiting behind it tells `createGoalDriver` (`BotRunner`'s `settled`, awaited before the bot is shown as idle): for a goal that is active it claims the next round (counted in one statement that holds to the cap) and wakes the bot with a note, `[note] Goal round N of M. Nobody is watching this round…`, which in the first round gives the objective and the checks and in later ones points to `get_goal`, so a long objective is not kept in the bot's memory once for every round; with no round left the goal is blocked and the bot is woken to tell the person. A round whose reply failed blocks the goal and the bot is told; any other reply that failed leaves it. On a shutdown the bots are stopped first, so replies that end because their runner stopped leave the goal active (not a failed round) and the next start takes it up. A tick every 30 seconds starts a round for an active goal whose bot is idle, as after the control plane was off or the computer offline, and a round that could not be started (no model, computer offline) is not counted. A person who writes in the middle of a goal is answered first, as the rounds only start when nothing waits.
- **Checks run on the bot's computer, in the runner.** `update_goal complete` runs each check with the computer's `exec_command` before it asks the control plane to complete the goal, and answers the bot with the failing ones, each with its command, how it ended (its exit code or a timeout) and the end of its output, as the harness does; a cancelled reply stops checking. The runner is trusted with this as it is with the bot's own words; the control plane does not run the person's commands.
- **Only the person's word starts or stops a goal.** A goal runs unattended for many rounds, and each round's note carries its objective and checks with the app's authority, so `start_goal` needs the person's own message, as `create_bot` does (`onPersonsWord` and not a request a bot passed on, since a page the main bot read may have written it); `pause`, `resume` and `clear` need a reply on the person's word, which the main bot may pass on (decisions 0065 and 0067). A scheduled run, a note and what another bot writes cannot start one, and a goal's own rounds cannot pause or clear it. `complete` and `blocked` are for the person's word or a round of the goal (the note that starts "Goal round", which only the app writes: `isGoalRoundNote`), not for another note, such as a scheduled task's, nor for another bot. Looking is for any reply. A round that has waited its turn behind the person's message ("pause it") and finds the goal no longer active is not worked.
- **Rounds are unattended.** A round is woken with a note, so `ask_question` is not offered, as for a schedule; the note says to message the person only for results or news.

## Consequences

- A goal is a series of ordinary replies of the bot, in its chat: what the bot says is there, and its memory carries the work from round to round (and is summarized past a size like any chat).
- Rounds follow one another as soon as a reply ends, with no pause, so a goal costs up to its round cap of full replies (20 unless asked for more, 100 at most); the cap, and the person's word to start, are what bound it. The round notes stay in the bot's memory, which is summarized past a size like any chat.
- A bot working a goal shows as at work in the sidebar for as long as its rounds go on; the person's own message waits behind the round that is running.
- The control plane must be running for rounds to go on; a goal in progress when it stops is taken up again by the next tick.
- There is no page for goals yet: the person asks the bot, whose `get_goal` says where it is, and pauses or clears it by asking.
